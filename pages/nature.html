Why Octopuses Punch Fish

Octopuses sometimes punch fish. And it isn't always because they're hungry.

| Marine Biology | | 1 min |

It turns out octopuses sometimes hunt alongside fish, particularly groupers. The fish flush prey out of hiding, while the octopus catches it. But cooperation doesn't always go smoothly.
When a fish takes more than its share or simply gets in the way...an octopus may reach out and smack it with one of its arms.
Scientists have even observed octopuses striking fish seemingly to control where they move during these hunts.
It's a temper problem. 🐙

Where did the Dinosaur-Killing Asteroid Hit

The asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs left a crater — and we know exactly where the crime scene is.

| Asteroid Impact | | 2 min |

66 million years ago, an asteroid roughly 10–15 kilometres wide slammed into Earth.
It triggered massive fires, tsunamis and a cloud of debris that changed the planet's climate; contributing to the extinction of around 75% of Earth's species, including the non-avian dinosaurs.
But we know where it hit.
The impact created the Chicxulub crater, a structure roughly 180 kilometres wide buried beneath Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula and partly beneath the Gulf of Mexico.
You can't simply look down and see it. Millions of years of rock, sediment and geological activity have hidden the crater from view.
Scientists discovered evidence for it in the 1970s, and later drilling and geological studies confirmed that the enormous buried structure was the result of an asteroid impact.
So somewhere beneath the Yucatán Peninsula is the scar left by the event that ended the age of the dinosaurs.

03

Ants That Farm

Humans aren't the only species that figured out farming. Some ants have been doing it for millions of years.

| Animal Behaviour | | 2 min |

Some ants don't just collect food. They farm it.
Leafcutter ants, for example, cut pieces of leaves and carry them back to their colonies. But they don't actually eat the leaves. Instead, they use them to grow a fungus.
The ants carefully maintain their underground gardens, feeding the fungus, removing unwanted organisms and protecting it from contamination. The fungus then becomes the colony's food source.
Even stranger, some ants also “farm” aphids, protecting them from predators in exchange for honeydew — a sugary liquid aphids produce.
So while humans were developing agriculture, ants were already running their own tiny farms underground. 🐜
